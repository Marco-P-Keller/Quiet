Quiet is a self-control tool, not an Instagram client. It enforces a daily time limit on a site the user already uses: minutes are chosen, spent while browsing, and when they run out the app closes for the day. Lowering the limit takes effect immediately; raising it is allowed once every seven days and starts the following day, which is the point of the app.

How the limit is reached: Quiet draws its own bottom row over Instagram's page. In its middle slot, where Instagram's row has Reels, Quiet puts a clock that opens its panel: today's minutes, the limit and Settings. The clock appears about ten seconds after launch; a double tap hides it, a tap on the empty slot brings it back. The screenshots show it hidden. Nothing in the app is switched on remotely or after review.

New in 1.1, off until the user turns it on: the limit can also reach the Instagram app. Settings › The Instagram app › "Limit the Instagram app too" asks for Screen Time authorization (FamilyControls, individual), then shows Apple's FamilyActivityPicker, where the user picks Instagram. A DeviceActivity monitor extension reports each minute the picked app is used; that time counts against the same daily limit as Quiet, and once the limit is spent a ManagedSettings shield, with a ShieldConfiguration extension saying why and until when, closes the app until 4 a.m. Quiet sees only opaque tokens and minute counts; nothing leaves the device. Turning it off takes effect the next day, like raising the limit; revoking Screen Time access in iOS Settings ends it at once. Without Instagram installed, any app can be picked to test it. Needs iOS 17.4.

Ratings: the only prompt is Apple's own requestReview, at most once per version, after five minutes of use across three days, never over the end-of-day screen. Settings › About has a "Rate Quiet in the App Store" row that opens the App Store's write-review page when tapped.

It shows Instagram's own mobile website in a web view and adds no social features of its own (no posting, messaging, moderation or accounts), so all content, reporting and blocking are Instagram's, through Instagram's own interface. Sign-in happens on Instagram's own page, in a separate web view with none of the app's stylesheet or script and no message channel back to the app, under the line "Quiet runs no code on this page." Before the first sign-in one screen says so and names two places to check it: iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › App Privacy Report, and Instagram's Accounts Center › Where you're logged in. The app never reads a password. Navigation is restricted to Instagram's and Meta's sign-in domains; anything else opens in Safari.

The app calls no Instagram API. There is no backend and no scraping, and no networking code of its own: every request is a page the web view navigates to or one Instagram's page makes in the user's session. The injected script only hides Reels and Explore, as a content blocker does, and reads the unread count Instagram's page already shows on its messages icon, to show it in Quiet's row; it is sent nowhere. The user agent is mobile WebKit's with "Quiet/1.1" appended. No advertising is hidden.

We are not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Instagram or Meta; the app says so on its About screen and in the description. The name, icon and subtitle use no third-party trademark.

To see the end-of-day screen quickly, set the limit to its minimum of 5 minutes and leave the app open.
